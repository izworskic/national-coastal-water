1. Official risk first

The National Weather Service beach forecast can include rip-current or swim risk, surf, winds, weather and water temperature. A High or Moderate category is never downgraded because an offshore buoy looks calmer.

2. Observations explain what changed

NDBC stations report measured waves, wind and water temperature when those sensors are available. The tool shows station distance and a roughly three-hour change rather than pretending an offshore station is the exact beach.

3. Tides explain timing, not safety

NOAA CO-OPS predictions show the next high and low tides near tidal coasts. Great Lakes water levels are not treated as tides, and missing tide data does not become a favorable signal.

4. Local rules still win

Beach flags, closures, lifeguards, emergency instructions, storms, shoreline structures and local currents can matter more than a regional forecast. This tool does not declare swimming or boating safe.

Decision boundary: Source proximity is not location eligibility. This tool first requires nearby NWS beach-forecast coverage, then organizes observations and predictions. It does not create a universal recreation-safety score, infer conditions where data is missing, or substitute a distant buoy or tide station for a coastal location.
